Privacy Policy.
Your messages stay yours.

Inkbox is an SMS app that sorts your messages on your phone. This page explains what the app reads, what it stores, what can leave your phone and how you can remove it.

  • Processed on your phoneSorting, verification code detection, spam filtering, search and the dashboard run on the device.
  • No server of oursWe do not run a server that receives your data, and we cannot see your messages.
  • No ads, no analyticsYour data is not sold, and it is not used for advertising or profiling.
  • Backups are your choiceA backup goes to your own Google Drive or a folder you pick, only after you set it up.

Who we are

This policy applies to the Inkbox app for Android (application ID app.utilio.inkbox), published by Utilio. For any privacy question, write to utilio.apps@gmail.com.

What Inkbox accesses

When you make Inkbox your default SMS app and allow access, it works with the following information on your phone:

  • SMS messagesThe text, phone numbers or sender names, dates, delivery status and the SIM used. This is needed to show, receive, send, sort and search your messages. Messages can contain sensitive details such as verification codes and bank transaction alerts.
  • ContactsOptionalNames and photos, to label conversations and to choose a recipient. Inkbox does not keep a copy of your address book.
  • SIM informationThe active SIMs and carrier names, to show and choose which SIM a message uses.

Inkbox handles SMS only. It does not process picture messages, group MMS or RCS chats.

If you stop using Inkbox as your default SMS app, it stops using the SMS access that depends on that role.

What is stored on your phone

Your messages stay in the Android SMS store. Inkbox also keeps its own data in the app's private storage:

  • A search index containing message text and sender numbers.
  • Categories, folders, sender rules, blocked numbers and your corrections.
  • Transaction details read from bank and payment messages, such as amount, merchant and reference, for the dashboard.
  • Drafts, scheduled messages, templates and your settings.
  • A diagnostics log of technical events without message content, verification codes, full phone numbers or passwords.

Numbers you block may also be added to the Android system block list.

The app's database is protected by Android's app sandbox and your device's own encryption. Inkbox does not add a second layer of encryption to it.

What can leave your phone

  • Messages you send go through your mobile carrier, as with any SMS app. Your carrier's terms and charges apply.
  • Backups are sent to Google Drive or written to a folder you choose, only if you set this up. If the folder you pick is synced by another service, that service receives the file.
  • A diagnostics report leaves your phone only if you export it and share it yourself.
  • Email to us: if you write to us, we receive your email address and what you send, and use it to reply.

A backup contains your messages, categories, rules, blocked numbers, drafts, scheduled messages, templates and settings.

Android's own cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off for Inkbox's data.

Google Drive backups

If you connect Google Drive, you sign in through Google's own screen. Inkbox asks for access to its private application data folder only (drive.appdata). It cannot see your other Drive files. It shows the address of the connected account so that you know where backups go. It never receives your Google password.

Inkbox keeps only the latest verified backup in that folder. It can back up automatically once a day, and when you ask. Google handles your account and stored files under the Google Privacy Policy, and Google's sign-in components may process data as described there.

Information received from Google is used only to provide backup and restore. It is not transferred to anyone else, not used for advertising, and not read by us. Inkbox's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Sharing and selling

We do not sell your data. We do not share it with advertisers, data brokers or analytics providers. The app contains no advertising or analytics software. Because your data is not sent to us, we have none to hand over to others.

Security

  • Connections to Google use HTTPS.
  • Backup encryption is on by default. Encrypted backups are protected with a key derived from your passphrase (Argon2id) and encrypted with AES-256-GCM.
  • We cannot recover a forgotten passphrase. Without it, an encrypted backup cannot be restored.
  • If you turn encryption off, anyone who can open the backup file can read your messages.
  • Screenshots and screen recording of the app's screens are blocked.

Notifications can show message content. You can control what appears on your lock screen in Android settings.

Keeping and deleting data

Data stays until you delete it. Messages in Trash, including expired verification codes moved there, remain until you permanently delete them or empty the Trash.

  • Messages: delete them in Inkbox, then delete them permanently from Trash.
  • App data: Settings, Privacy, Delete app data removes Inkbox's categories, rules, search index and other data. SMS messages stay on the phone, messages in Trash show again, and numbers on the Android block list stay blocked.
  • Uninstalling removes the app's private data. It does not remove SMS messages or backup files.
  • Local backups: delete the files from the folder you chose.
  • Google Drive backups: turning Drive off in Inkbox stops new backups but keeps the existing file. To delete it, open Google Drive on the web, go to Settings, then Manage apps, and delete Inkbox's hidden app data.

Inkbox has no user accounts of its own. Email you send us is kept only as long as needed to handle your request, and you can ask us to delete it.

Children

Inkbox is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
